Good morning everyone. Today we are going to continue our discussion of gradient descent. Last week we talked about the loss function, and how we measure the difference between the model's prediction and the true label. This week, I want to focus on how we actually minimize that loss. The basic idea is simple. We compute the gradient of the loss with respect to each parameter, and then we take a small step in the opposite direction. The size of that step is called the learning rate. If the learning rate is too large, the training will diverge. If it is too small, training will take forever. In practice, most people use an optimizer called Adam, which adapts the learning rate for each parameter automatically. Another important concept is the mini batch. Instead of computing the gradient over the entire data set, we sample a small batch of examples, maybe thirty two or sixty four, and estimate the gradient from those. This is called stochastic gradient descent. It is noisy, but it is much faster, and the noise actually helps the model escape bad local minima.
